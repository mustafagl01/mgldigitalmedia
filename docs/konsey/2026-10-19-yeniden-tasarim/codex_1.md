## Core metaphor

**“The handoff map”: every job moves from an incoming signal, through system work, to a clearly marked human decision.**

This becomes the site’s main content component—not decoration. Visitors switch between examples such as “quote request” and “appointment enquiry,” then see:

`Request → collect/check → prepare action → human approval → send/update`

Each stage reveals the apps involved, what MGL builds, what remains under client control, and the relevant package.

## Homepage

About seven sections is the estimate:

  Headline: **“Systems that move work forward—until a person needs to decide.”**  
  Supporting copy: “MGL builds automation, websites, advertising systems and AI reception for UK and Turkish businesses.”

- **Choose your starting point.** “I manage operations” / “I run a local business.” These change examples, not the underlying offer.

## Visual system

Proposed exact design tokens:

- Porcelain: `#F5F6F2`
- Carbon: `#171A1F`
- Cobalt: `#3155D9`
- Pale signal blue: `#DDE5FF`
- Verified green: `#197052`
- Functional warning red: `#B83A4B`

Cobalt represents active paths; green appears only at verified or approved stages. The site remains predominantly light.

Use **IBM Plex Sans** for interface and body copy, paired with **IBM Plex Mono** for workflow states, prices and technical annotations. Both support Turkish glyphs and make the system feel operational rather than generically “luxury.”

Redesign the logo. Keep the recognisable `mgl ai` name, remove the orange italic treatment, and replace `/ SYSTEMS` with a restrained modular descriptor. The current logo would contaminate the new palette.

## Signature interaction

Build a **Human Checkpoint Simulator**. The visitor selects an incoming task, then advances it through a short, deterministic workflow. At each step the system declares:

- what is automated;
- what data is used;
- where approval is required;
- what happens after approval;
- which package supports it.

It must be an explanatory simulation, never a fake live dashboard. Motion level: **calm**—path tracing, state changes and restrained transitions; no scroll theatre.

## Trust without case studies

- Link directly to Companies House and show the registered identity plainly.
- Publish the real coffee-shop project with its exact scope—no unsupported performance claims.
- Label every demo “Concept demonstration—not client work.”
- Provide downloadable sample deliverables: workflow map, implementation plan, approval matrix and support handover.
- Show redacted automation walkthroughs using fictional data.
- Publish clear boundaries: ownership, cancellation, usage charges, support and data retention.
- Explain where human approval remains mandatory.
- Apply the existing **14-day** promise only where its terms genuinely fit; do not imply that a completed bespoke website is universally refundable.
- Use a real founder photograph and a concise, non-inflated biography.

I would remove the hair-transplant demo from featured work. It creates an ethical conflict and makes the portfolio look like interchangeable agency templates.

## Image plan

- Hero: no generated image; the real workflow simulator is the visual.
- Automation overview: an orthographic paper-like handoff system connecting inbox, documents, approval and output.
- Operations path: a dispatch desk with quote sheet, inventory record and approval card.
- Local-business path: booking request, phone, calendar and payment checkpoint.
- AI assistants: voice and message inputs converging on one human-controlled queue.
- Websites: real screenshots from the live coffee-shop site and honestly labelled demos.
- About: real founder photography only.

Shared style: cool-white miniature architecture, cobalt routing lines, crisp directional light, subtle grain, no photorealistic people.

Forbidden: humanoid robots, glowing brains, floating fake dashboards, neon circuitry, fake client premises, invented metrics, stock-photo smiles, orange gradients, or AI-generated founder/client portraits.

## What would make it fail

Preserving all existing routes, hiding prices, presenting demos as proof, making the simulator look live, or cramming every service into the hero would fail. So would oversized cinematic assets: the brief-supplied decision window is under sixty seconds, and many visitors arrive on mobile.

The largest strategic risk is pretending a one-founder company has agency-scale capacity. Position it honestly as senior, owner-led and deliberately bounded—with clear onboarding limits and response expectations.
