### 1. Is the current hero suitable?
**No.** It suffers from an identity split. It tries to be an index for four distinct offerings (via 4 service tiles) while forcing visitors into an entry door for only *one* of them (automation doors + simulator). Stacking a tag, H1, lede, four tiles, a sub-label, and two door buttons creates severe visual fatigue. Crucially, the visual and H1 still anchor 100% on automation, signaling to prospective web or ads clients that their needs are merely side hobbies.

---

### 2. Umbrella Promise vs. Listing Services

**Keep one umbrella promise.** Listing four disparate services in an H1 produces a cluttered grocery list. The "customer journey" framework succeeds because it aligns client acquisition with operational control. 

**Does the approval metaphor hold?** Yes. Across websites, ads, AI receptionists, and automations, the client’s underlying anxiety is loss of control or wasted spend. The approval gate proves that the system captures demand and handles the heavy lifting, but never acts recklessly without owner oversight.

* **EN H1:** Get found, answer every lead, and automate the work—with you in control.
* **EN Lede:** High-performing websites and ads to bring customers in; AI receptionists and custom automations to prepare the work until you give the green light.
* **TR H1:** Müşteriye ulaşın, her talebi karşılayın, işi otomatikleştirin—kontrol sizde kalsın.
* **TR Lede:** Sizi öne çıkaran web ve reklam çözümleri; nihai onayınıza gelene kadar süreci yöneten yapay zekâ asistanı ve otomasyonlar.

---

### 3. Hero Structure (Max 5 Elements)

Keep the first screen uncluttered and decisive:

1. **Tag:** `UK & Türkiye · Small and large businesses`
2. **H1:** Customer journey umbrella promise.
3. **Lede:** Explicitly naming the four disciplines and the approval gate.
4. **Primary Action Group:** Two side-by-side buttons: `Explore 4 Services` (smooth scroll) and `Try the Simulator`.
5. **Background Visual:** The full-journey miniature diorama.

**Placement of tiles, doors, and simulator:**
* **4 Service Tiles:** Move directly to **Section 2** (immediately under the fold). Giving them their own clean band gives Web, Ads, AI, and Automation equal dignity without suffocating the hero.
* **2 Automation Doors + Simulator:** Position them directly beneath Section 2 as the deep dive into your operational engine. The doors belong strictly as the intake selector for the simulator, not floating independently in the hero.

### 5. One Risk
**Dilution of specialist positioning.** Expanding the umbrella promise to accommodate web and ads risks making the business look like a generic, fragmented digital agency rather than a high-margin, bespoke automation and systems firm.
