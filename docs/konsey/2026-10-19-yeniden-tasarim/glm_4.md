**First, honestly:** I can't open `hero3.png` from here — I'm judging from the written spec, which matches the crowding described. Position stated accordingly.

## 1. Is the current hero suitable?

**No.** Three tells: the H1 is automation's promise promoted to brand promise; the image tells automation's story (rooms → one node); even the lede's grammar subordinates the services — "websites, ads, AI" is a list wedged inside an automation sentence ending on the approval clause. The owner reads the page correctly: it looks like an automation company that also does other things. Plus seven stacked elements is too many for one screen regardless of content.

## 2. H1: umbrella — but a different one

Disagree with the owner's instinct that the hero should *explain* all services: an H1 that enumerates four services becomes a menu, not a promise. But I also disagree with keeping approval as the umbrella — that's exactly what caused this round. Approval can *close* a journey; it can't open one ("approval of what?"). The customer-journey umbrella works, and it rescues approval as the final beat, the green-only rule, and the simulator. So: the metaphor survives, demoted from headline to destination.

**EN**
- H1: **"From the first search to your approval, the work runs itself."**
- Lede: "Websites and ads that get you found, an AI phone and WhatsApp assistant that answers every enquiry, automation that runs the work — nothing reaches a customer until you approve."

**TR**
- H1: **"İlk aramadan onayınıza kadar, iş kendi kendine yürür."** *(bonus: "arama" means both search and call — the double meaning covers get-found and answer in one word)*
- Lede: "Sizi site ve reklamlarla bulurlar; yapay zekâ asistan her aramayı yanıtlar, otomasyon işi yürütür — onayınız olmadan hiçbir şey müşteriye gitmez."

## 3. Structure

First screen, exactly five elements: **tag / H1 / lede / one CTA pair / image**. CTAs: "See it work" (scrolls to simulator) + "Talk to us."

- **4 tiles → out of the hero,** into the section immediately after ("Four ways in…"), one line of honest proof each (12 live sites; live ads client; etc.).
- **2 doors + simulator → their own section after the tiles**, not directly under the hero. Directly-under-hero repeats the old mistake in a new location: automation eats screen two before websites and ads get their beat. Rename the label "Where would you start?" — dropping "with automation" is the whole point of this round.

Order: hero → four services → simulator. That sequence *is* the journey.

## 4. Image: regenerate, keep the style

Keep the miniature-architecture language; the scene changes from "three rooms converge on a node" to "four stations along a route."

**Desktop 16:9, empty left 45%:** the right half is a miniature street of four modules joined by one dotted cobalt line running left to right — a lit storefront with an oversized magnifier-shaped doorway (found), a small phone-counter with a tiny assistant figure answering (enquiries), workshop rails with cobalt conveyors carrying tiny jobs (automation), and finally a small desk where a human figure stamps, lit in the only green on the page. The connecting line lets the journey read in a single glance.

**Mobile 9:16, empty top 40%:** the same four modules stacked vertically as simplified silhouettes, the dotted line running top to bottom, green desk at the bottom — order preserved, detail stripped.

## 5. One risk

A café owner who just wants a website may read "end-to-end journey" as *systems integrator, not for me* and bounce before scrolling. Mitigation: the tiles section opens with "Start with any one — they connect if you want them to," and each tile links straight to its own page. The umbrella must feel like a menu of doors, not a package deal.
