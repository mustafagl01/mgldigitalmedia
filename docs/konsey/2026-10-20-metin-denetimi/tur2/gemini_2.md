### Overall Voice & Style Takeaways

1. **Eliminate Literal Translation Calques**: The copy is rife with phrases translated word-for-word from English (*"gönder tuşu sizde"* from *"the send button is yours"*; *"sonraki adımı yazalım"* from *"we'll write the next step"*; *"nakit sıkıntısı olarak döner"* from *"comes back as a cash crunch"*). Replace them with authentic, idiomatic Turkish business expressions.
2. **Complete the Sentences (Eliminate Verbless Fragments)**: Several key headings and explanatory lines drop their finite verbs (*"Kurulum £2,950’den başlayan"*, *"Aynı işler, her gün yeniden"*, *"şartları yazılı"*). While short feature lists can be noun phrases, marketing explanations and legal commitments must have complete predicates to sound reliable.
3. **Bridge the Register Gap (Salon Owner vs. Factory Director)**: The voice currently oscillates between overly casual colloquialisms (*"müşteriyi kovalamak"*, *"sonra durur"*, *"bu pakette yok"*, *"çıktıyla dönelim"*) and stiff, unfeeling legalese (*"konu size bırakılır"*). Establish a warm, confident, professional B2B tone that respects both an artisan's daily pressures and a plant manager's need for rigor.
4. **Unify Cross-Page Terminology**: Inconsistent nomenclature undermines trust. Standardize product names across pages (e.g., unify *"Sesli Asistan"* vs. *"AI telefon asistanı"*, consistently use *"e-posta"* instead of mixing with *"mail"*, and use standard compliance terms like *"anonimleştirilmiş"* instead of *"isimleri gizlenmiş"*).
5. **Turn Passive Wishes into Active Imperatives**: Many headers and callouts rely on passive or optative verbs (*"Aramalar karşılansın"*, *"Bulunun, talep alın"*). Replace them with confident, benefit-driven active verbs (*"Hiçbir çağrıyı kaçırmayın"*, *"Daha çok bulunun, daha çok talep toplayın"*) that inspire direct action.
