### Overall Voice and Style Recommendations

1. **Purge Developer Terminology from Client UI:** Eradicate raw software and database jargon like *ayrıştırıldı* (parsed), *dinlenir* (listener/webhook), *satır eklenir* (row appended), and *form doldu*. Replace them with natural business expressions such as *bilgiler çıkarıldı / netleştirildi*, *takip edilir*, and *kayıt olarak işlenir*.
2. **Eliminate English Syntactic Calques:** Phrases like *Gelen teklif talebi, incelemeye hazır bir taslak olarak* (dangling sentence fragment), *Tek bir darboğaz*, and *sonra durur* read like unedited machine translations from English marketing wireframes; Turkish demands complete predicates and smooth causal transitions.
3. **Harmonize Casual and Industrial Audiences:** Slangy expressions like `Müşteri "geliyorum" der`, `İşi kuran kişi`, or `araçlara bakın` undermine credibility for factory directors, while staccato legal fragments (*sayılmaz*, *en az 3 ay*) feel curt to salon owners. A calm, warm, consultative B2B tone (*...doğrudan kurucu uzmanla görüşürsünüz*, *...dahil edilmez*) bridges both worlds naturally.
4. **Transform Passive or Pleading Labels into Professional Verbs:** Standalone card labels like *Yorum isteme* (feels like begging for reviews), *Talep yakalama* (too aggressive/technical), and *Bulunun* (passive command) should be replaced with confident, value-oriented terms like *Google Yorum Toplama*, *Talep Karşılama*, and *Web Varlığı*.
5. **Complete Elliptical & Telegram-Style Predicates:** While brevity is good, chopping off verbs (*aynı kişi*, *sonra durur*, *en az 6 ay*) creates an unpolished, rushed impression. Completing standard grammatical predicates (*aynı kişidir*, *asgari sözleşme süresi 6 aydır*) preserves conciseness while establishing trust.
